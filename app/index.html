Model mechanics

Separate regularized log-linear regressions for 24, 48 and 72-hour offsets. Features: log rainfall, rainfall × regime weights, mean humidity, sea-level pressure, wind, latitude, longitude and month.

Regime weights remain transparent routing proxies, not learned weather diagnoses: four scores begin at 1, with +3 for dry (rain <1 mm), depression-like (pressure <1005 hPa and wind >15 km/h), orographic proxy (elevation >700 m and humidity >75%) and monsoon-like (humidity >75% and rain ≥1 mm). Scores are normalized. Regression coefficients are learned, not hand-set.

Output = max(0, exp(features · coefficients) − 1). No probability or calibrated confidence is inferred. The raw GFS forecast remains visible.

    Indian-network rainfall evaluation

    Exact zeros. Better rainfall errors. Warnings still withheld.

    Test period for the 48h and 72h rows: July 1–September 24, 2026. 2,064 location-days per lead across 24 points. Metrics use ERA5 rain reanalysis as reference. Adjacent sites and repeated weather events are correlated; these are not 2,064 independent storms.

    Update, October 6, 2026: the 24h model was refit on a longer history. The 24h forecast now uses an India-wide QM+XGBoost model fitted on about 4.7 years of data (4,000 grid points, 7 regions, 2021-03-25 to 2025-08-31, 2,013 days; two days with a corrupted NOAA file are skipped) plus a heavy-rain flag, with no v1 blend. The 48h and 72h forecasts still use the v2 ensemble described below. The 24h row (*) is a different evaluation set from the other rows: 376,000 point-days from the 4,000-point network, June 28 to September 29, 2026, monsoon only, all after the fitting period. On it, MAE is 5.59 vs 6.38 for raw GFS and RMSE 11.62 vs 14.13, and the flag finds 1,668 of 5,027 heavy days (raw GFS 1,205) with 2,840 false alarms (raw 2,868). About 2 of 3 heavy days are still missed. On sites never used in fitting, 24h MAE is 2.86 vs 3.31 and false alarms 411 vs 518, but heavy hits are 170 vs 175 of 947 for raw GFS, so the flag does not beat raw GFS on heavy days at unseen sites. The previous 24h model (fitted on 998 days), refit with the same recipe on this evaluation set, scored MAE 5.71 and 1,512 heavy hits, so the longer history helped modestly. Research product, not an official warning.

    Live ensemble (48h and 72h): QM+XGBoost weights are 22.5% and 20% for 48h and 72h; the remainder is v1. Exact raw rain zero is forced to zero. In this reused exploratory evaluation the v2 ensemble at 48h beat v1 and raw GFS on both MAE and RMSE. 72h RMSE (17.713) remains worse than raw GFS (17.338), though better than v1 (17.950). This known tradeoff was accepted for this research release. None is near-perfect. Across raw-zero cases, ERA5 references still had at least 1mm on 151 / 142 / 147 days; the gate suppresses those missed-rain cases. ERA5 is reanalysis, not measured gauge truth.

    Training, validation and spatial holdouts

    Fit: January 1–August 31, 2025, 4,374 rows per lead from 18 locations. Original v1 validation: September 2025 plus January–June 2026. Ensemble validation: September 2025–June 2026, 3,798 / 3,798 / 3,762 rows for 24 / 48 / 72h, selecting frozen QM+XGBoost/v1 blend weights. QM and XGBoost were fitted only on training rows. Test: July–September 2026, excluded from coefficient fitting and regularization selection. This is an exploratory development evaluation: aggregate test metrics were inspected during development, so it is not a pristine independent benchmark. Six sites were excluded from fitting and validation to test geographic transfer: Shimla, Jodhpur, Raipur, Shillong, Bengaluru and Port Blair.

    All 24 sites have nine months of 2025 and nearly nine months of 2026. Raw archive: GFS fixed lead-time series from Open-Meteo Previous Runs, not the near-initialization historical archive. Hours within a day can come from different runs. Live inference uses the latest GFS run and nearest daily lead model, so the live/archive lead alignment is approximate and the published scores are archive scores, not prospective live skill.

    Scope: a representative regional network, not full-grid India, not district-level validation, and not proven nationwide generalization. Some seasons, especially late-year northeast-monsoon conditions, are not covered. Stations and independent observations are needed before safety use.

    More event detection. Still too many false alarms.

    These candidates are not the live correction or an operational warning system. 24h and 48h rainfall candidates improve RMSE but worsen MAE versus original v1; the 72h candidate also loses to raw GFS on RMSE. No candidate passed the warning release gate. The live warning stage still abstains. The live rainfall amounts now use the QM+XGBoost/v1 ensemble described above, not these warning-research candidates.
